Notice This

Paul uses a single compound Greek verb, synestaurōmai (perfect passive), meaning a decisive death in the past whose liberating consequence continues every morning you wake up.

Christian identity is not self-improvement; it is exchange of center—Christ living inside your actual Tuesday and Thursday.

בָּרָא bārā · Strong’s H1254

The verb is a Qal perfect, third person masculine singular — a simple, completed action, stated as plain fact. What sets bārā apart in the Hebrew Bible is that its subject is exclusively God; humans make (‘āśāh) and form (yāṣar), but only God brings the new out of nothing.

Houtōs gar ēgapēsen ho Theos ton kosmon hōste ton Huion ton monogenē edōken hina pas ho pisteuōn… echē zōēn aiōnion
μονογενῆ monogenē · Strong’s G3439

This adjective is often translated “only begotten,” a rendering going back through the Latin Vulgate’s unigenitus. Morphologically, it joins monos (“only”) with genos (“kind, class”) — meaning “unique, one of a kind, the beloved Son without peer.”

Verb · Qal Perfect · 3rd Person Masculine Singular
BSB gloss: “created” · Subject in Qal/Niphal is exclusively God
✦ Eliana word study — from Genesis 1:1

In Hebrew, craftsmen shape existing clay or timber, but bārā is reserved exclusively for divine creation—initiation without preexisting resistance. Notice that the same verb returns when David asks God to create a clean heart (Psalm 51:10) and when Isaiah proclaims the new heavens (Isaiah 65:17).

02 · Fresh Rendering

“For God loved the world in this way: he gave his one-and-only Son, so that everyone who believes in him would not perish but have eternal life.”

03 · Deeper Lens
μονογενῆ (monogenē) — The Unrepeatable Son

In Greek, monogenēs combines monos (“only, single”) and genos (“kind, stock”), echoing the Septuagint’s translation of Hebrew yāḥîd in Genesis 22:2 (“Take your son, your only one, whom you love”). What Abraham was spared at Moriah, the Father completes here.

04 · Everyday Insight

The opening word Houtōs means “in this manner” rather than merely “so much.” God’s love is not an abstract mood measured by a thermometer; it has a concrete shape in history—a gift given before we asked (Romans 5:8).

05 · Take-Home Practice

Today, when you catch yourself trying to earn God’s approval, pause and name one concrete place where you can rest in a love already given.

✦ Eliana · Spiritual Companion Context: John 3:16 & Chain №3
“Eliana, how does monogenēs in John 3:16 connect back to Abraham and Isaac in Genesis?”
✦ Eliana: In the Hebrew of Genesis 22:2, God calls Isaac Abraham’s yāḥîd — his “only, cherished son.” When Jewish translators rendered Genesis into Greek, they chose agapētos and monogenēs (see Hebrews 11:17). So when John 3:16 says God gave ton Huion ton monogenē, every reader steeped in Scripture hears Moriah in reverse: the Father Himself provides the Beloved Son.
